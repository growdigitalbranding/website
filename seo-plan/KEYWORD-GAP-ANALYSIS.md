# Keyword Gap Analysis — top 10 competitors

**Date:** 2026-09-27 · **Site state:** commit `0d00dd3` (7 target keywords optimised)

---

## Method, and what this can and cannot tell you

**What was done.** 20 live web searches. Ten were run on the target keyword set
and adjacent topics to identify who actually occupies the results. Ten more were
restricted to one competitor domain each, to list the pages that competitor has
indexed. Every competitor claim below is traceable to a URL in the Sources list.

**What this is.** A *coverage* gap analysis: which keyword themes each
competitor has a dedicated, indexed page for, and whether growdigitalbranding
has one. That is the part of a gap analysis that decides what to build.

**What this is not.** It has **no search volumes, keyword difficulty or ranking
positions**. Three limits caused that:

1. No SEO data API (DataForSEO, Ahrefs, Semrush) is connected.
2. The search tool runs from the US. An Indian query returns Indian pages, but
   in US order, so **appearing in a result set is evidence and position is not.**
3. Competitor pages could not be fetched. The egress proxy blocks direct
   access, so page depth and word counts were not assessed; only titles and
   URLs were.

Figures quoted from competitor pages (retention rates, lead counts, conversion
rates) are **their claims, unverified**, and are reported only to show what
they compete on.

## 4. The gaps, prioritised

Priority is judged on three things together:

- how many competitors hold the theme
- how close it sits to buying intent
- whether this site has a genuine right to the topic

A gap is not worth closing if closing it means pretending to be a different
agency.

### P1 — build these first

| # | Gap | Held by | What to build | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **WhatsApp marketing for real estate** | SA, IN, TS ×2, WD, plus 4 vendors | **Retarget `/what-we-do/follow-up-systems`** to own "WhatsApp marketing for real estate builders", with a matching article | The most crowded theme outside the core keywords. You already have the substance: the follow-up service and the speed-to-lead article. It is a retitle and an expansion, not a new capability. |
| 2 | **Portal leads vs own paid leads** (99acres, MagicBricks, Housing.com) | DM | Article: *Portal leads or your own ads: the cost-per-booking comparison* | Only one competitor holds it, and it is exactly the arithmetic this site already does for channel partners. Very high fit, low competition. |
| 3 | **Bengaluru city page** | SA (plus 9 other cities) | `/real-estate-marketing-agency-bangalore`, using both spellings | You claim Karnataka, mention it 5 times, and "Bangalore" appears zero times. **Build only if there is real Bengaluru work to describe.** `/work` already lists a Bengaluru villa engagement, so there appears to be. |
| 4 | **Pre-launch marketing for real estate** | IN | Article or page: *Pre-launch marketing for a real estate project* | The substance exists, split between the `/what-we-do` FAQ and the launch-vs-sustenance section. It needs one URL that owns it. |
| 5 | **Named case studies** | DM, TS, MJ | 1–3 case studies at their own URLs | Already the top item in the SEO plan. Three competitors publish named projects. Still blocked on client permission. |

### P2 — next quarter

| # | Gap | Held by | What to build |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | NRI marketing | DM | Article or page on marketing a project to buyers abroad. The Google Ads page already explains the location-interest setting; this expands it. |
| 7 | Real estate SEO | PP, TS, JT | Article: *Does SEO work for a real estate project?* Keep it an article unless SEO is a service you actually sell. |
| 8 | Lead funnel / landing pages | SA, DM | Article: *What a real estate landing page has to do*: speed, tracking, form friction. It links to the CAPI and tracking pages. |
| 9 | Creative examples | IN | Article built on your own ad creatives, cleared ones only. The 19 in the homepage marquee are an asset no competitor has shown. Leave out any that carry real third-party brand names. |
| 10 | "How to choose an agency" | PP, MJ, SQ | A buyer's guide rather than a self-ranking listicle: the questions to ask any agency, with published pricing as the first one. It captures "best real estate marketing agency" intent honestly. |

### Deliberately not recommended

| Theme | Why not |
|---|---|
| **Programmatic city pages** (the BrandYodha pattern) | Doorway pages for cities you do not operate in. They are a quality liability and the opposite of this site's position. |
| **Realtor / broker / US pages** (SA, DM, PP all have them) | Wrong buyer. This site sells to developers, and that focus is part of its credibility. |
| **Social media, email and video "strategy" posts** (WD, IN, DM) | These are generic, crowded and off-positioning. One of them would not move the needle, and ten would dilute a site that wins on specificity. |
| **Website design** (JT, PP) | Only if it is a service you actually sell. |

## 5. Where you are ahead — protect these

Seven themes surfaced **no competitor page at all**, and this site owns every
one:

1. **Published pricing.** No pricing page surfaced for any of the ten. BrandYodha's page says pricing "varies".
2. **Server-side tracking and CAPI** as a dedicated service, with a live `/tracking-setup` proof page.
3. **RERA requirements in ad creative.**
4. **Creative volume and fatigue**, with a calculator.
5. **Speed to lead** as a standalone, derived argument.
6. **Free calculators.** Three of them, ungated.
7. **Cost per booking** as the reporting unit. Several competitors say "quality leads"; none has a page built on the arithmetic.

On AI search visibility, Insomniacs and Pitch Pine each have blog posts; this
site has a service page and a long-form article.

These are the pages most worth promoting and earning links to. They are what a
competitor cannot copy in a week.
